The Collections Loop

Mafadi and Ripple run through four manual handoffs and one fragile browser session. Here is how that becomes a closed loop that builds its own next round — and what the dashboard on top of it should actually show.

Diagnosis

Five steps, and where each one breaks

Every campaign runs the same way today. None of these failures are the voice platform's fault, and none are the skills' fault — they all trace back to one thing, which is the section after this.

The pivot

Stop pulling from Jobix. Make Jobix push to you.

Every problem above is a symptom of the same root cause: the call data never lands anywhere you own. It lives in someone else's dashboard behind a session cookie, so every campaign starts with a scrape.

You already built the fix and haven't switched it on. Speed to Lead has a live call-ingestion endpoint on the MPM form:

What it acceptsWhat that gives you
POST /api/forms/{form}/callsOne request per call attempt — answered, voicemail, busy, failed. Not one per contact.
suidLinks the attempt straight back to the tenant record you imported. No phone-number matching, no dedupe guesswork.
transcriptRole-tagged turns with timestamps, stored next to the record. The transcript-verification step stops being a fetch loop and becomes a column.
event_idDuplicate webhook deliveries are discarded on arrival. Retries are safe.
attempt, final, duration_seconds, recording_urlAttempt decay and the dead-number pile become queries. Recordings play inline.
importBatchId filter on call_statsAlready the campaign key. Connect rate, contacts reached, attempts per contact, talk time and a per-outcome breakdown, per campaign, already computed.
Live finding

call_stats on the MPM form currently returns total: 0. The endpoint is built, authenticated and schema-validated — and not one call has ever been posted to it. The whole pipe exists and nothing is going through it.

Wire the post-call webhook to that endpoint and Phases 0 through 3 of the analyser — workspace check, paging, transcript fetch, reach classification — stop being work you do. They become a query against your own database.

If Jobix has no outbound webhook, write the sync worker instead. Same destination, but headless: service credentials, on a cron, polling conversations and posting each one into Speed to Lead. It is the code already sitting in the skill, moved somewhere it can run unattended. A day's work, and it retires the browser step permanently.

Everything below this is easy once call data lands in a database you control, and effectively impossible while it doesn't.

Architecture

Six layers, four of which you have already written once

Intake

Client file in, import out

A watched inbox or Drive folder per client. The existing builder script runs headless on arrival and emits two things: the import file, and a data-quality sheet — dead numbers, phones failing the SA mobile-prefix check, duplicate tenants with their combined exposure, floats that got mangled.

Surfaces the data problems before anyone dials. You currently find them after the campaign.

Dispatch

Batch out, receipt back

Push the import, record the returned batch id against the campaign, then reconcile unique phones dialled against records loaded. If the gap isn't zero, alert.

The silent-79 detector. It is a subtraction.

Truth

One classifier, versioned and tested

The definitions currently living in a skill prompt, moved into code: reached, the dedupe rank, first-reach decay, arrears under commitment versus cash committed with its floor and ceiling.

The model then reads and writes about the numbers instead of recomputing them.

Surface

Two screens, not one

A client-facing campaign view carrying the eight KPIs already validated with Mafadi, and an operations view you'd actually open daily. Detailed below.

Both read the truth layer, so the dashboard and the Word report can never disagree.

Redial

The next round builds itself

The Phase 5 exclusion rules as a scheduled job. Round N+1 exists the morning after round N closes, with an attempt cap and a dialling-hour spread taken from your own answer-rate data.

A person approves the round instead of assembling it.

Report

Scheduled, not requested

The report-maker skill stays exactly as it is — branding, section order, house wording — but fires on campaign close and takes its figures from the truth layer rather than re-deriving them.

The five-point verification checklist becomes assertions that fail a build.

Screen two — the operations view

Redial engine

The rules that decide round N+1

Phase 5 of the analyser already encodes this. As a nightly job it stops being a task and becomes a queue waiting for approval.

DispositionWhoWhy
ExcludedAny stop outcomePromise to Pay, PTP Recommitted, Partial Payment Arrangement, Paid Claimed, Dispute Logged, Escalated, Office Visit Claimed, Callback Requested, Wrong Number, Refused to Pay.
ExcludedSpoke, made no commitmentThey answered and declined. Calling again burns goodwill; this belongs in the exception queue, not the dialler.
Excludeddo_not_call, internal test lineCompliance, and the test number that has appeared in every campaign.
ExcludedOver the attempt capSet the cap from the decay curve, where new unique reaches flatten out.
ReroutedDead numbersEvery attempt connected for zero seconds. Goes back to the client for a phone number, not into another round.

Note

Build this from the exclusion list rather than the inclusion list, exactly as the skill does today. It's shorter, and it makes every skipped tenant auditable — you can show a client why someone wasn't called again.

The gap

From promised to actually paid

Everything above measures commitments. Paid Claimed is the tenant's word, taken on a phone call. The number that sells this service is recovered rands, and it cannot be computed from call data alone.

The fix needs one thing from each client: a monthly receipts or payments file. Reconcile PTPs against actual receipts and you get kept, broken, partial, and paid-without-a-call — which yields a PTP-kept rate per client, per agent prompt, per campaign.

That's the metric worth building toward. It turns a pipeline figure into a recovery figure, and it's the only honest way to answer whether a prompt change made things better. Until a client sends receipts, be explicit in the reports that commitments are commitments.

Scaling

Two clients, one system, config not code

Mafadi and Ripple are the same shape with different details: column maps, brand colours, outcome vocabularies, agent prompts, report sections. Right now that difference is expressed as two separate skills whose builder scripts are largely the same script.

Everything client-specific belongs in a config file — column mapping, outcome ranks, stop list, palette, report section order, dialling hours. Client three should then be a config, a form and an agent prompt. Not a rebuild, and not a third skill to keep in sync with the first two when a pitfall gets fixed.

Build order

What to do in what order

Genuinely sequential — each phase is cheaper because the one before it happened. Phase one pays for itself before anything else is built.

Phase 1

Get the calls into your own database

Wire the Jobix post-call webhook to the Speed to Lead calls endpoint, or write the cron sync worker if there's no outbound webhook. Then backfill the campaigns you've already run, so the history is queryable too.

Unlocks: the browser step disappears. Nothing else on this page needs to exist for that to be worth it.

Phase 2

Lift the classifier out of the prompt

Port reach, dedupe, decay and the commitment split into code, with the eight fixtures above as tests. Point the report-maker at it instead of at a fresh derivation.

Unlocks: figures stop drifting between the dashboard and the report, because there's one source.

Phase 3

Put a surface on it

Operations view first — batch health, dead numbers, exception queue, transcript search — because that's the screen you'd use daily. Client view second, since the Word report already covers that audience.

Unlocks: campaign state without asking anyone, including mid-campaign.

Phase 4

Close the loop

Redial engine on a schedule, next round generated and held for approval. Intake watcher on the client folders. Reports fire on campaign close.

Unlocks: campaigns run continuously instead of when you sit down to run one.

Phase 5

Reconcile payments

Once a client sends receipts, PTP-kept becomes the headline metric and everything above becomes provable rather than promised.

Unlocks: recovery rate per client and per prompt — the number that wins the next client.

Scope

What stays human, deliberately

Approving an import before it dials. Disputes and escalations. The tenant who has been retrenched. Dead numbers going back to the client rather than into another round. A prompt change, after you've seen why it's needed.

Automating collections isn't removing the person. It's making sure the only things that reach them are the things that need them — which is exactly what the exception queue is for, and why it's on the screen you open first.
